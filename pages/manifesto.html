01 — The premise

Your software should be made for you.

Not for the median user. Not for the segment most likely to convert. Not for the persona that came out of last quarter's research. For you. The person who will open this thing tomorrow morning and use it.

02 — The problem

Most software is made for someone else.

For investors, for retention curves, for support deflection, for partner integrations, for the launch deck. By the time it reaches you it has been compromised by every audience it serves except yours.

03 — The wager

An audience of one is liberating.

No growth target. No SLA. No ten-thousand-person median to optimize against. The cost of starting is one folder. The cost of quitting is one rm -rf. You can finally build the version of the thing that's exactly right.

04 — The practice

Build the one version that fits.

The to-do app that reads better at night. The drinking-water tracker that nags in your own voice. The reading list ordered the way you actually pick books off the shelf. None of them venture-fundable. All of them achievable in an afternoon.

05 — The invitation

Start here.

This page lives in a personal lab. Adding a new page is one file. Adding a new app is one folder. Deleting either is the inverse. The infrastructure paid itself off the moment you stopped not-starting things.
